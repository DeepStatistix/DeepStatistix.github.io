Research agenda

One question across domains: which forecast can we trust?

Markets, rivers and stocks are connected, seasonal and prone to shocks. My work asks which model suits which series, how to measure accuracy honestly, and how to put forecasts and their uncertainty in front of the people who decide.

01

Model choice per market

No single model wins everywhere. I compare statistical, machine-learning and deep models series by series, and combine them through ensembles and stacking.

02

Evaluation that mimics deployment

Chronological hold-outs, walk-forward validation, Diebold–Mariano tests and interval coverage. Accuracy only counts if the test resembles real use.

03

From model to decision

Forecasts with ranges and what-if scenarios for growers, traders and agencies, plus open-source tools that make correct inference the default.

Flagship · HADP, SKUAST-Kashmir

Market Intelligence Cell (MIC)

A research programme on horticultural price forecasting under the Holistic Agriculture Development Programme. It asks which model works best for which market, and it publishes forecasts with ranges for growers, traders and agencies.

78series · 25 markets
9 / 11model families won at least one series (2024 hold-out)
93%interval coverage, live 2026 cherry season

What the research found

  • No universal winner. On the 2024 hold-out, 9 of 11 model families were best for at least one series (LSTM 6, Transformer 5, TBATS 5…).
  • Complexity is not a guarantee. The Transformer was best on one series and last on 35 of 78.
  • Live test, 2026 cherry season: model forecasts had an error of 18.3% against 27.5% for the naive benchmark, and beat it on 11 of 13 series.
  • Custom stacking, tuned per market, gave the lowest error in all six markets in my PhD thesis (MAPE 1.1–3.0%).
